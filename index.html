Graphene transferPMMA spin coatingRamanDIC imagingPythonQC

One good sensor is easy. Reliable ones at scale is the fun part.

  • Developed chip fabrication methods, including graphene transfer onto polymer substrates and PMMA spin coating of graphene-on-copper to form the support layer for transfer. Optimizing the PMMA film raised post-transfer chip yield from 50% to 90%.
  • Scale manufacturing processes from R&D to production.
  • Established three in-house graphene transfer methods (thermal release tape, electrochemical delamination, chemical etching), eliminating dependence on commercial transfer vendors.
  • Run and contributed to the design of a two-stage QC pipeline: Raman spectroscopy to verify graphene quality before transfer, and robot-automated DIC imaging after transfer to confirm graphene integrity on the chip.
  • Built a Python image-review tool for pass/fail classification of biosensor images, with results stored in a database used to remove defective sensors; also develop Python tools for data compilation and process analysis.
  • Train new team members on manufacturing protocols and equipment; maintain records in eLabFTW.

Pre-transfer QC. Raman spectroscopy confirms graphene is present and checks its quality from the characteristic G, 2D and D peaks before it is transferred onto a chip.

Post-transfer QC. A Python review tool I built displays each robot-captured DIC image of a biosensor so the reviewer can classify it as pass or fail.

Every classification is compiled into a database that is used to screen out defective sensors.

Three in-house graphene transfer methods, ending dependence on commercial transfer vendors.

Chemical etching: the copper growth foil is dissolved, leaving the PMMA-supported graphene film for transfer onto the chip.

Electrochemical delamination: the PMMA-supported graphene is lifted off its copper foil electrochemically instead of dissolving the foil.

Thermal release tape: a PMMA-free dry transfer in which graphene is picked up on thermal release tape and released onto the substrate with heat.
