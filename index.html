Below is an example of an Opentrons protocol I wrote in Python, for liquid deposition in a non-standard layout. For each of 35 positions on a 7 × 5 grid, the pipette picks up a fresh tip, aspirates 100 µL from a 15 mL tube, and dispenses five 20 µL drops in a 4 mm-diameter ring before discarding the tip. After deposition, the five drops merge and cover the entire target area. A built-in simulation test at the end of the script checks every step before the protocol runs on the robot.

Graphene transferQCRamanDIC imagingPythonPMMA spin coating

One good sensor is easy. Making reliable ones at scale is the fun part.

Where I focus in production:

  • Develop graphene transfer and chip fabrication methods and run a two-stage QC pipeline (Raman before transfer, robot-automated DIC imaging with Python review after). Optimizing the PMMA support layer raised post-transfer chip yield from 50% to 90%.
  • Scale manufacturing processes from R&D to production.
  • Develop Python tools for data compilation and process analysis.
  • Train new team members on manufacturing protocols and equipment; maintain records in eLabFTW.

I defined the requirements for, and run, a two-stage QC pipeline.

Stage 1, pre-transfer QC: Raman spectroscopy confirms graphene is present and checks its quality from its characteristic spectral pattern and peaks before it is transferred onto a chip.

Stage 2, post-transfer QC: robot-automated DIC imaging confirms graphene integrity on each chip. A Python review tool I built displays each image so the reviewer can classify the biosensor as pass or fail.

Every classification is compiled into a database used to screen out defective sensors and to feed multidimensional analysis of how defects affect sensor sensitivity and yield.

For graphene transfer onto polymer substrates, I use PMMA spin coating of graphene-on-copper to form the support layer, and I established three in-house transfer methods that eliminate dependence on commercial vendors:

1. Chemical etching: the copper growth foil is dissolved, leaving the PMMA-supported graphene film for transfer onto the chip. This is the most time-efficient protocol, with minimal handling of the graphene layer, preserving its integrity.

2. Electrochemical delamination: the PMMA-supported graphene is lifted off its copper foil electrochemically instead of dissolving the foil. This preserves the copper for recycling and reuse, while minimizing the graphene's exposure to highly oxidizing copper etchant.

3. Thermosensitive tape-based transfer: a dry transfer in which graphene is picked up on thermal release tape and released onto the substrate with heat. No PMMA and no chemicals such as copper etchant are involved.

How I build these tools:

  • Design custom lab tools in Fusion 360 (CAD).
  • Prepare and print parts with Bambu Studio on a Bambu Lab P1S 3D printer with an AMS (Automatic Material System).

Gripper, adapter and top. Designed in collaboration with an automation engineer, with my focus on structural stability. It lets the assembly robot pick up and press FAST™ plates. The adapter couples the gripper to the robot's mounting pattern without intruding on the clearances the sensor needs as it approaches, and its solid structural design minimizes bending under pressing force.

Seyonic pressure regulator enclosure. Houses the pressure regulator of the Seyonic pipetting system. Sized to fit the regulator closely while leaving its connected tubing and electrical wiring unconstrained, with hollow sections in the body to save material.

Micropipette rack. Wall-mounted rack that holds three micropipettes upright, regardless of their size, keeping them off the bench and within easy reach.

Serological pipette controller. Wall-mounted holder that keeps a serological pipette controller upright. Its nozzle sits in a close-fitting cup that holds it steady.

Circuit board rack for vacuum oven. Designed to fit the maximum number of circuit boards into the limited space of a vacuum oven, while keeping enough separation between boards for easy retrieval and to prevent board-to-board scratching.
